\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:introduction}
% =============================================================================

Explainable AI (XAI) attribution methods are critical for understanding neural network decisions. However, almost every ``which attribution method is best'' conclusion was established on small CNNs and never re-validated on the Vision Transformer (ViT) foundation models that now dominate practice. This work delivers a controlled, statistically rigorous benchmark that measures attribution \textbf{fidelity} across three orthogonal axes: \textit{architecture} (CNN vs ViT), \textit{pretraining objective} (supervised, self-supervised, masked, contrastive), and \textit{model scale} (small to giant) \cite{brandt2023precise}.

The main contributions of this paper are:
\begin{itemize}
    \item A comprehensive, controlled benchmark of XAI attribution methods across ViT foundation models.
    \item Three empirical findings regarding method transferability, pretraining effects, and metric disagreements.
    \item An open-source, Quantus-based evaluation harness for the community.
\end{itemize}

\section{Methodology and Benchmark Design}
\label{sec:methodology}
% =============================================================================

\subsection{Research Questions}
This benchmark is designed to answer the following:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{RQ1 -- Transfer:} Do attribution-method fidelity rankings established on CNNs hold on ViT foundation models?
    \item \textbf{RQ2 -- Pretraining:} Does the pretraining objective change attribution faithfulness?
    \item \textbf{RQ3 -- Scaling:} How does fidelity scale with model size?
    \item \textbf{RQ4 -- Attention vs The Rest:} Do attention-native methods beat gradient/perturbation methods on ViTs?
    \item \textbf{RQ5 -- Metric Agreement:} How much do faithfulness metrics agree with each other?
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Attribution Methods Under Test}
We evaluate Gradient methods (e.g., Integrated Gradients), CAM methods (e.g., Grad-CAM++), Attention-native methods (e.g., Attention Rollout), and Perturbation methods (e.g., LIME, Occlusion).

\subsection{Evaluation Metrics}
Following the Quantus framework, we measure faithfulness (e.g., Faithfulness Correlation), localization (e.g., Pointing Game, AUC), and robustness.
